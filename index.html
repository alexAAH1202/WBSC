The Program

One connected learning system

Modern international softball moves faster than the mechanics built to officiate it once did. Sightlines shift mid-play. Batters and baserunners generate speed that compresses the time an umpire has to find the right angle, and defensive positioning has grown more varied, so plays develop in ways that reward anticipation as much as reaction.

The 2026–2029 Umpire Mechanics Program was revised to meet that reality. Every module in this pathway is built around one question umpires ask before every pitch — not only where to be, but why that position gives them the view the play requires.

That question runs through the whole system, but it is answered most directly in two documents.

Appendix 4 provides the why. Appendix 5 provides the how.

Particular attention must be given to Appendices 4 and 5 and their accompanying presentations. Appendix 4 establishes the philosophies and principles behind elite mechanics. Appendix 5 applies those principles to advanced field mechanics.

Softball Australia Guidance

Mechanics Bulletin MB-2026-1 applies this framework within the Softball Australia pathway: Elite mechanics (Appendices 4 and 5) are used at U18, U23 and Open National Championships, and are assessed for umpires at Levels 5 and 6. Umpires at Levels 1–4 continue to be trained and assessed on Foundational mechanics — Elite mechanics are introduced once an umpire has a strong command of the foundational material, not as a starting point.

Other Federations should confirm the equivalent guidance for their own accreditation pathway.
